Is race real? Our DNA experment

In my 9th-grade New Media and Technology class, we were assigned to groups with names corresponding to different colors. I was in the red group. We were tasked with collecting our DNA and duplicating it a billion times, then comparing our results with our classmates'. The most important steps are DNA extraction and PCR setup. DNA extraction involved using a micropipette to add 50 microliters of extraction buffer to a small tube. Then we used a toothpick to collect the DNA in our mouths, and then transferred it into the tube. Finally, we incubated the tube for 10 minutes at 95 degrees Celsius. This last step transitions into the first step of PCR setup. Before putting our tube in the miniPCR machine, we first added PCR reagents to a bigger 200 microliter tube. When testing, we looked for a specific part of our DNA called a VNTR (Variable Number Tandem Repeat in genetics) due to the variation. Some people repeat 10 times, and some 50. Because humans are so similar, we tested a variable that's known to have variety, and tested if classmates who identified as the same specific races are more closely related than those who don't. Lastly, we wrote a CER paragraph arguing whether race was biologically real or socially real. This is what I argued:

"Race is not biologically real. In our experiment, we used a toothpick to collect our DNA, then extracted it using a micropipetting technique. Then we used PCR amplification with a heat block to rapidly create a billion copies to then compare them to others. Students who identified as different races often shared a genetic overlap. For example, a student who identifies as Black and Hispanic matched closely with a student who identifies as white and Hispanic. #7 identified as Black or African American, yet matched with #3, who identified as Hispanic. The total average of people who identified as the same ethnicity was 30. The number who matched with others who were of a different ethnicity was 242. The question, “Is race biologically real?” is really asking whether race is a social construct or not. Race has been categorized into specific groups for years now. Biologically speaking, many scientists have rejected the idea of race being a biological aspect of someone rather than a social construct. Some may argue that humans who share phenotypes such as a similar amount of melanin in their skin. The reason why scientists reject the idea of race being real biologically is that people with a similar amount of melanin can still have great genetic variation. Unlike animal species, all humans share 99.9% of DNA. If you compare a person who was born in North Africa to a person born in Southeast Asia, while their phenotypes will be different, they will still share a significant amount of DNA with each other. If biological races were real, we would have observed that people who identified as Black/African American would have matched DNA-wise. However, this did not happen. Most participants shared an overlap of sorts. Ex: A person who identified as White and Hispanic may have matched with someone who identified as Black/African American."
